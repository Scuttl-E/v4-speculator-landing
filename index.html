What the model does

Compare the position you have with the exposure you want.

This calculator models and creates expected Peapods V4 position profiles, and compares them with alternative ways of holding the same capital. It can be used to compare V4 with spot, lending and perpetual positions, while the Harvester models scheduled withdrawals from the V4 position as price targets are reached.

The model is built from the V4 mechanics and examples Peapods has made public to date: leveraged LP exposure, dynamic borrowing, automatic LTV rebalancing, cashback products and Volatility Farming. V4 remains under development and is not yet fully documented, so some assumptions may need to be refined as additional specifications and live performance data become available.

Price-only base curves

How to read the displayed results.

The displayed V4 curves are price-only base curves. They do not add Volatility Farming yield or subtract borrowing, rebalancing and execution costs. Peapods’ design uses volatility-generated yield alongside automatic rebalancing, with that yield intended to offset or exceed the costs of maintaining the leveraged position—funding the mechanics of the strategy while also providing additional yield to the position. The model therefore treats those effects as broadly neutral at the base-curve level rather than attempting to estimate an unknown future net yield. Actual V4 performance may ultimately sit above or below these curves.

Scenario analysis

Three positions. Three different reasons to rethink the trade.

Each example converts the user’s current net equity into V4 and follows both positions across the same range of ETH price moves.

Scenario A · Spot asset holder

Stay exposed to the bull case. Build the exits in advance.

Current position & standpoint

10 ETH$15,000 at a $1,500 ETH price

He believes ETH can reach $10,000 within his time horizon, while accepting that it may first trade back toward the $1,000 level. He wants to remain positioned for that full upside range rather than reduce his exposure early.

Goals

  • Maintain maximal ETH exposure until the $10,000 target is reached.
  • Take profits at $4,000, $5,000, $7,000 and $8,500.
  • Keep liquidation risk low by avoiding traditional leverage.

Modelled V4 position

50% allocated to a 2.5× Long + 50% allocated to a 2× Short, with cash selected as the routing mode.

The structure is designed to combine defensive downside behaviour with increasingly convex upside exposure.

Scenario B · Lending position

Remove the liquidation cliff without abandoning the upside.

Current position & standpoint

20 ETH$30,000 collateral · $15,000 debt · $15,000 net equity

The position liquidates at 85% LTV—approximately $882 ETH. He is prepared to hold for the long term and expects ETH may eventually reach $7,500, but considers a move below $1,000 possible first and has no immediate plan to sell.

Goals

  • Hedge the existing position and prevent liquidation through the expected lower range.
  • Preserve as much upside as possible if the longer-term target is reached.
  • Release an additional $3,000 in the most capital-efficient way available.

Modelled V4 position

60% allocated to a 2.5× Long + 40% allocated to a 2× Cashback Short, releasing $3,000 in cash.

The short-side component is used to defend the lower range while the larger long allocation protects the bullish objective.

Replace the cliff

The original position ends at $882. The V4 curve does not.

The lending comparator reaches its liquidation boundary at a −41.18% ETH move. The modelled V4 position continues across the range, preserving $11,040 at −60% and $17,160 at −80%.

02

Accept a narrow trade-off

Slightly behind first. Increasingly ahead later.

At +100%, V4 is $42,000 versus $45,000 in the lending position. By +200%, V4 is ahead; at +400%, the model shows $234,600 versus $135,000. The short-side defence is paid for by a small early-upside gap.

Scenario C · Perpetual long

Stop defending margin. Keep a credible route into the bull case.

Current position & standpoint

20 ETH long$1,800 entry · $1,500 current price · $13,200 margin

The position carries a $6,000 unrealised loss, leaving $7,200 of current net equity and approximately 4.17× effective exposure. Liquidation sits at $1,200. He remains long-term bullish but no longer wants to keep defending the margin position.

Goals

  • Protect the remaining equity against another adverse move.
  • Maintain as much bullish exposure as possible without the supplied perp liquidation boundary.
  • Recover capital on an upturn for use in future speculative perp positions.

Modelled V4 position

100% allocated to a 2.5× Long using the position’s current $7,200 net equity.

The comparison deliberately rebases onto the equity that remains today, rather than the original margin.
01

Trade the liquidation line

Less immediate upside than the perp. Far more room to be wrong.

The existing perp liquidates at $1,200. At that same −20% move, the modelled V4 position retains $4,608. Spot is included to show how much exposure would be surrendered by simply closing into unleveraged ETH.

02

Rebase onto current equity

The comparison begins from the $7,200 that remains.

The model converts current net equity—not the original margin—into a 2.5× V4 long. The original perp leads through the early upside range, while V4 progressively closes the gap without carrying its supplied liquidation boundary.

Recover the margin

Extract $13,200 over six periods, then let the remainder run.

The fastest-capital-recovery plan harvests 20% at each checkpoint and restores the full original margin by +180%. At +500%, $156,828 remains active and total wealth reaches $170,028.
