Model scope

A baseline for price response.

The displayed V4 curves are price-only base curves. They do not add Volatility Farming yield or subtract borrowing, rebalancing and execution costs. At this base-curve level, the model is also time-independent and path-independent: a given asset price move produces the same modelled result regardless of how long it takes to occur or the price path taken to reach it.

In practice, the path matters. Peapods’ design uses volatility-generated yield alongside automatic rebalancing, so a more volatile route to the same final price may generate additional yield that the base curve does not capture. That yield is intended to offset or exceed the costs of maintaining the leveraged position, while potentially adding further return.

The displayed curves should therefore be read as a structural baseline for price response, not an estimate of total realised return. The model is built from the V4 mechanics and examples made public so far, and its assumptions may need refinement as fuller specifications and live performance data become available.

Scenario analysis

Three positions. Three different reasons to rethink the trade.

Each example converts the user’s current net equity into V4 and follows both positions across the same range of ETH price moves.

Scenario A · Spot asset holder

Stay exposed to the bull case. Build the exits in advance.

Current position & standpoint

10 ETH$15,000 at a $1,500 ETH price

The user believes ETH can reach $10,000 within their time horizon, while accepting that it may first trade back toward the $1,000 level. They want to remain positioned for that full upside range rather than reduce their exposure early.

Goals

  • Maintain maximal ETH exposure until the $10,000 target is reached.
  • Take profits at $4,000, $5,000, $7,000 and $8,500.
  • Keep liquidation risk low by avoiding traditional leverage.

Modelled V4 position

50% allocated to a 2.5× Long + 50% allocated to a 2× Short.

The structure is designed to combine defensive downside behaviour with increasingly convex upside exposure.
01

Shape the range

Convex upside with a defensive short component.

This is more than downside protection. The 2× Short can become profitable enough on a severe ETH decline to push the combined position above its starting value, while the 2.5× Long increasingly dominates on the way up. The result is a position designed to extract value from both directions while retaining strongly convex upside.

02

Inspect the construction

Turn the curve into concrete outcomes.

The detailed view shows the position’s exact value, return from entry and V4 edge over spot across the planned price range. At −40%, V4 retains $11,200 versus $9,000 in spot; at the $10,000 target, it reaches $362,360 versus $100,500, a V4 edge of $261,860.

03

Harvest the route

Harvest $81,325 while keeping the remaining V4 position active.

Four checkpoints match the planned profit targets. By the $10,000 ETH target, the plan has harvested $81,325 while leaving $140,585 still active in V4, for $221,910 total wealth. The remaining V4 position alone is still $40,085 ahead of holding spot at the target.

Scenario B · Lending position

Remove the liquidation cliff without abandoning the upside.

Current position & standpoint

20 ETH$30,000 collateral · $15,000 debt · $15,000 net equity

The position reaches its 85% LTV liquidation threshold at approximately $882 per ETH. The user is prepared to hold for the long term and expects ETH may eventually reach $7,500, but considers a move below $1,000 possible first and has no immediate plan to sell.

Goals

  • Hedge the existing position and prevent liquidation through the expected lower range.
  • Preserve as much upside as possible if the longer-term target is reached.
  • Release an additional $3,000 in the most capital-efficient way available.

Modelled V4 position

60% allocated to a 2.5× Long + 40% allocated to a 2× Cashback Short, releasing $3,000 in cash.

The short-side component is used to defend the lower range while the larger long allocation protects the bullish objective.
01

Replace the cliff

The original position ends at $882. The V4 curve does not.

The lending position reaches liquidation after a 41.18% decline in ETH, fixing a hard endpoint for the trade. The modelled V4 position remains active through the lower range, retaining $11,040 at −60% and rising to $17,160 at −80% as the short component increasingly benefits from the deeper decline.

02

Accept a narrow trade-off

A modest early cost for a widening V4 edge.

The defensive structure creates a modest cost on the earlier upside: at +100%, V4 is $42,000 versus $45,000 in the lending position. By +200%, that gap has reversed; at +400%, V4 reaches $234,600 versus $135,000, a $99,600 V4 edge.

Scenario C · Perpetual long

Stop defending margin. Keep a credible route into the bull case.

Current position & standpoint

20 ETH long$1,800 entry · $1,500 current price · $13,200 margin

The position carries a $6,000 unrealised loss, leaving $7,200 of current net equity and approximately 4.17× effective exposure. Liquidation sits at $1,200. They remain long-term bullish but no longer want to keep defending the margin position.

Goals

  • Protect the remaining equity against another adverse move.
  • Maintain as much bullish exposure as possible without the supplied perp liquidation boundary.
  • Recover capital on an upturn for use in future speculative perp positions.

Modelled V4 position

100% allocated to a 2.5× Long using the position’s current $7,200 net equity.

The comparison deliberately rebases onto the equity that remains today, rather than the original margin.
01

Trade the liquidation line

Less immediate upside than the perp. Far more room to be wrong.

The perp offers the strongest near-term upside, but another 20% ETH decline wipes out the remaining equity. Spot removes that risk but gives up leveraged upside. V4 achieves the middle ground: at the same −20% move it retains $4,608 while preserving substantially more upside potential than spot.

02

Rebase onto current equity

Rebase the decision on the equity that remains.

The original perp leads through the early upside range, but V4 keeps building without its liquidation boundary or ongoing funding drag. That makes a longer horizon viable and shifts the focus toward recovering capital along the way.

03

Recover the margin

Recover the original $13,200, then keep the remaining position working.

With the longer horizon established, six 20% harvest checkpoints recover the full original $13,200 margin by +180%, progressively taking capital back off the table without closing the position. At +500%, the remaining V4 position has almost caught the original perp on its own, while total wealth has already moved ahead, without the liquidation cliff or ongoing funding drag.
